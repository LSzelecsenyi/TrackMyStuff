Privacy policy

Effective date: 8 November 2026. This notice describes the Strict Android app and the Strict account service in this version. It is not a legal certification.

Who is responsible

The controller is Szelecsényi László, a natural person, of 2141 Csömör, Bajcsy-Zsilinszky utca 1., Hungary. The privacy contact is laszlo.szelecsenyi@gmail.com. No data protection officer is appointed.

Strict is local-first, and sign-in is required

Workout history, plans, the exercise catalog, body weight, measurements, progress photos, goals, and achievements stay on the phone. Strict does not upload that log as a cloud copy. Google sign-in is mandatory, so Strict does process account information on its server.

Data on your phone

You enter training data in the app. A starter exercise list can be created on the phone at first launch. The app stores it in a local database, private photo files, and on-device preferences. Progress photos are not added to the public gallery. Theme, language, and lock-screen preferences are device settings.

Health Connect is optional. If you allow it, Strict reads recent steps, exercise sessions, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, and sleep duration to show them. Those readings are not written into Strict’s database, are not sent to the Strict service, and are not included in a ZIP backup. Strict does not write them back to Health Connect.

Google account and sessions

  • The app requests a Google ID token. It does not request Gmail, Drive, or contacts. The token is sent to the Strict service to open a session and is not stored as the session itself.
  • The server stores the Google subject as the account key, and the email address when Google says it is verified.
  • The display name is returned at sign-in and kept in the on-device profile. It is not written to a user-name column on the server.
  • The server assigns a Strict user id.
  • The phone stores an opaque session token in encrypted app storage that Android backup excludes. The server stores only a SHA-256 hash of that token. The default session lifetime in the current configuration is 30 days. There is no refresh token.

Purpose: recognise the account and keep Founder access, promotional access, and a verified purchase with that person.

Founder Program

If you join, the server stores the application and, for qualifying workouts, limited metadata: completion time, local date, workout name, duration, exercise count, completed-set count, whether it came from a template, and whether it used external load. A tester report can include the text you write, the app version, and the platform. That upload does not include exercise names, individual sets, reps, weights, notes, body weight, photos, or Health Connect data.

Promotional Pro access

Founder, Early Adopter, Pro Discovery, and Welcome Back are separate programs. Each can be active, paused, or permanently retired on its own. Pausing or retiring Founder does not start Pro Discovery or Welcome Back. Pro Discovery uses the Strict account registration date and existing promotional history to decide when an offer can be shown. No workout activity synchronization is required. While the account is eligible, it can receive a Pro offer every two months. The first offer can start one 14-day trial. A later offer does not start another free trial. The server stores the trial activation and expiry, if a trial was used, and the time an offer was shown. Welcome Back stores the qualifying workout id you send, activation, expiry, and a cooldown. An existing grant stays valid when its program is paused or retired. A new grant is not issued while that program is paused or retired.

If you delete the account after using a one-time benefit, a marker records which benefits were used so the same Google account cannot take them again while that promotion can still be issued. The marker is a keyed HMAC of the Google subject plus those flags. It is pseudonymous, not anonymous. It does not store the subject, email, name, or workout content. When a promotion is permanently retired, that promotion's flag can be removed automatically. The marker row is deleted only when no flag is still needed. Pausing a program, or filling its capacity, does not remove the marker.

Google Play subscriptions

Paid checkout is not offered until Play products are configured and a real purchase has been tested. When a purchase is verified, the server stores the purchase token, a hash of the token, package name, product id, subscription state, expiry, and order id. Google Play also processes the payment.

Deleting a Strict account does not cancel a Google Play subscription. Billing stays with Google until you cancel it in Google Play. The purchase is unlinked from the deleted account. A purchase that Google Play still treats as entitled, including a purchase made before Strict sent an account tag, can be verified onto one new Strict account. The same purchase is not active on two Strict accounts at the same time. The server keeps the purchase token, a hash of the token, package name, product id, subscription state, expiry, order id, and the time the subscription ended.

The token stays while the subscription is entitled or can still return to an entitled state, including a pause, account hold, or grace period. After the subscription has ended, the purchase row, including the token and order id, is deleted once 24 calendar months have passed from that end. Cancellation time is not the start of that period while paid access continues. A retention hold can keep one row past that date. That hold is not applied automatically. This notice does not say that 24 months meets a tax, accounting, or dispute duty. Play notification ids are kept so a repeated notification is not applied twice. Those ids do not contain the purchase token or the Strict account.

Feedback, backups, and logs

Send feedback opens your email app with a draft to laszlo.szelecsenyi@gmail.com. The draft includes the app version, Android version, and device manufacturer and model. Strict does not send the email. Your email provider handles it if you send it.

A ZIP backup contains the structured training data and progress photos you export. You choose the file location. Strict does not delete that file later. Health Connect readings and the session token are not in the ZIP. Android can also back up workouts, body data, and appearance and language settings when the phone’s backup settings and schedule allow it. The latest changes may not be included. Photo files, sign-in sessions, and Pro or special status are not in that Android backup. Android does not promise to remove an older cloud copy after you delete an account.

The production service runs on Oracle Cloud in Frankfurt, Germany. Public requests pass through Cloudflare before they reach that host. This notice does not state how long Cloudflare keeps logs.

On the host, Nginx keeps an access log and an error log. The access log stores the connecting IP address, an address sent in the X-Forwarded-For header, the request URL including a query string when one is present, the response status, the referrer, and the user agent. It does not have a field for the Authorization header or for cookies. The files rotate daily, and 10 rotated files are kept.

The application log is the system journal on the same host. It is kept in temporary storage and removed when the host reboots. Debug and trace logging are off. The application records error codes and rejection reasons. The sign-in diagnosis does not record the ID token, Google subject, email, or name. Purchase tokens are not written to those application logs. A review of the journal present on 8 November 2026 found no email address, Google subject, or access token. This notice does not say that a later error message can never include other text.

PostgreSQL does not log connections or ordinary SQL statements. A database error can be written to the database container’s log. A size limit for that container log is not turned on.

Some Oracle Cloud logging, including audit logs, network flow logs, and load-balancer logs, was not visible from the host. This notice does not say those logs are off.

A manual copy of the production database is stored on the Frankfurt host. Nothing on that host deletes it automatically. It can contain account, sign-in, session, and purchase records, including an account that was deleted after the copy was made. A daily automatic backup is planned and is not running. The planned rule would remove an automatic copy older than 20 days only after a newer copy has been verified, and it would keep the newest verified copy even if that copy were older than 20 days. That plan is not a promise that every database copy disappears after 20 days.

The app’s dependency list does not include an analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting SDK. It does include Play services libraries used for sign-in and billing. This notice does not describe telemetry from those libraries, and it does not state a transfer tool for Cloudflare, Google, or Oracle, such as standard contractual clauses or an adequacy decision.

How long data is kept

  • Training data on the phone remains until you delete it, delete the account, or uninstall the app. An Android backup, if the phone made one, can remain outside the app.
  • The server account, sessions, Founder records, and promotional grant rows are deleted when the account deletion succeeds. A database copy made before that deletion can still contain them until that copy itself is removed.
  • A one-time benefit marker remains while that promotion can still be issued. After the promotion is permanently retired, its flag can be removed, and the marker row is deleted when no flag remains.
  • An active or still-recoverable Play purchase remains so it can be verified onto one Strict account. An ended purchase row is deleted 24 calendar months after it ended, unless a retention hold is set.
  • Nginx access and error logs rotate daily, and 10 rotated files are kept. The application journal is removed when the host reboots. The database container log has no size limit in the current configuration.
  • The manual production database copy has no automatic deletion date. The planned daily backup is not running.
  • An exported ZIP remains wherever you saved it.

Account deletion

In the app, open Settings and choose Delete account. The button stays disabled until you confirm. The signed-in Strict session identifies the account. The app does not ask you to choose a Google account again. Local data for that account is removed only after the server confirms deletion. The same Google account can sign in later as a new empty account. Founder recognition, a used promotion, and the old workouts are not restored. A Play purchase that is still entitled can be verified onto that new account, and it cannot be active on two accounts at once.

If you no longer have the app, use Delete your Strict account on this site. That page verifies a fresh Google sign-in. It is separate from deletion in the signed-in app. That page is part of this server build. It is not a claim that the public production address is already online.

Sign-out and uninstall do not delete the server account. Deleting the account does not erase a ZIP you already saved, an Android backup the phone already made, or a database copy made while the account existed.

Your requests

You can delete individual measurements, photos, and workouts in the app, export a ZIP of local training data, and delete the account as described above. There is no separate self-serve download of the server profile.

To ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, or a portable copy of server-side data, email laszlo.szelecsenyi@gmail.com. Requests are handled manually.

Security

The release app is configured to call the API over HTTPS. The session token on the phone is encrypted, and the server stores a hash of it. The local database is in app-private storage. This notice does not claim a separate encryption layer for that database, and it does not claim a security certification.

Children

People aged 16 and 17 may use Strict, as may adults. Strict is not directed at children under 16. It has no separate child mode, it does not ask for a date of birth, and it requires a Google account.
